Labor and AI: A Simulation Exercise

Course: Research Strategy | Grade: Pending | Institution: Maastricht University

A simulation exercise accompanied by a report — not a research paper — building a synthetic data-generating process to study how AI adoption reshapes regional labor markets.

Project Overview

This exercise simulates 1,000 regions over 20 years to model the labor market dynamics of AI adoption, distinguishing it explicitly from prior robotics-automation literature. Using group-specific Cobb-Douglas production functions, the simulation imposes a displacement effect on high-skill workers alongside a complementarity effect on mid-skill workers, then tests whether standard difference-in-differences estimators can recover these imposed effects from staggered, and later continuous, treatment timing.

Technical Approach

The exercise compares OLS, Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE), the Sun & Abraham (2021) estimator, and a manually implemented continuous-treatment Callaway-Goodman Bacon-Sant'Anna (CGBS, 2025) estimator. A Goodman-Bacon (2021) decomposition is used to isolate the bias TWFE incurs from "forbidden comparisons" under staggered adoption, while the continuous DGP — where AI investment dollars replace a binary treatment indicator — tests each estimator's ability to recover dose-specific causal responses.

Tools: R, Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE), Goodman-Bacon Decomposition, Sun & Abraham (2021), Continuous-Treatment CGBS (2025).
